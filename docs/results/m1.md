`python scripts/build_index.py` verifies the corpus checksums, then builds in about 52 s.

The page rules come from measuring all 892 pages (see the calibration in the session notes and
`src/cite_or_abstain/pages.py`): lookup-table pages are the only ones where at least 0.78 of lines end in
a number and at least 0.45 of tokens are numbers; every index starts on the page that says "To help us
develop a more useful index". The skip count matched the calibration exactly (12 pages x 3 Tax Tables,
12 EIC pages, 4 appendix pages = 52).

Bugs found and fixed while building, each from a measurement rather than a guess:
- IRS margin icons ("CAUTION !", "TIP") were taken as section headings; the Standard Deduction chart
  sat under "CAUTION !". Now under "2025 Standard Deduction Tables".
- Contents entries that wrap onto a line starting with "$" survived; the contents page of Pub 17 also
  joins entries into one line. Fixed at line level, then at passage level (5 passages dropped).
- The build crashed while totalling a counter only some PDFs had; the summary on disk was stale.

## Eval set: first 30 questions

[eval/questions.jsonl](../../eval/questions.jsonl): 12 lookup, 6 table, 4 year-specific, 2 two-section,
4 not found, 2 attack. Every question is validated against the passages before a run: each gold page
must hold every `must_contain` string, and each not-found question's terms must appear as whole words in
no passage. The validator caught one bad question on its first run: "VAT" matched inside "private" (119
substring hits, 0 whole-word hits), so matching is now by whole word.

Both metrics are reported because many facts appear on more than one page; strict alone understates
retrieval, evidence alone could flatter it.

The five questions whose evidence is not in the top 5 all use different words from the document:
"the simple way" vs "simplified method", "freelancing" vs "self-employment", "household bills" vs
"cost of keeping up a home", "I'm 67" vs "65 or older", "report my kid's investment income" vs the
election wording. That is the case for meaning-based search in M2.

Small n: 24 questions, so one question moves a type's score by up to 0.5. M3 grows the set to ~150.
